The City Council voted 9 to 4 early Tuesday morning to approve a sweeping $2.4 billion transit plan that will extend the light-rail line along the riverfront, build three new stations, and reshape waterfront development for a generation. The decision, reached after nearly seven hours of public comment, ends eighteen months of bitter debate between neighborhood groups, business owners, and transit advocates.

Under the agreement, construction will begin next spring on the first phase of the extension, connecting the existing terminus at Harbor Street to the renovated Union Pier. Officials estimate the segment will carry 42,000 riders a day by its second year of operation, roughly triple the bus ridership it replaces. A second phase, still unfunded, would push the line north into the industrial district.

Opponents argued the project's cost had ballooned beyond what taxpayers were promised when the measure first appeared on the ballot. "We are being asked to mortgage the next thirty years for a line that serves a fraction of the city," said Council member Priya Raman, who voted against the plan. Supporters countered that delays have only made the project more expensive, and that federal matching funds will lapse if the city waits another year.

Construction impacts are expected to begin downtown within weeks, with lane closures on two major corridors. The transit authority said it will publish a mitigation plan by the end of the month, including shuttle service and small-business grants for merchants along the route.
